# Rack & File — UX and visual design review

Paste this with the screenshots in `design-review/`. Attach them in the order
they are numbered; the file names are referenced below.

## The brief

You are reviewing **Rack & File**, a personal training log. I want a design
review that makes it better looking, faster to get started with, and easier to
operate mid-set with one sweaty hand. Be blunt. I would rather hear that a
screen is wrong than hear that it is fine.

### What the app is

- A phone-first web app, installed to the home screen as a PWA. All the
  screenshots are 390 × 844 (iPhone-class). There is no desktop layout worth
  discussing.
- Offline-first and local-only: everything lives in the browser, there is no
  server in the training path, and an optional sign-in only backs the data up.
  It has to work in a basement gym with no signal.
- It follows a fixed seven-day rotation — two strength days, cardio days, a
  pool day, recovery — and you log sets against whichever day it is.
- A plan can also be generated by pasting a prompt into any chatbot and
  tapping the link the reply comes back with.
- The visual identity is gym equipment: cast-iron surfaces, chalk-white text,
  and the Olympic bumper-plate colours (red heaviest, then blue, yellow,
  green, white) as the accent. The accent changes per day, by how heavy that
  day is. Type is Archivo with Chivo Mono for numbers. There is now a light
  palette as well as the dark one.

### What I want reviewed

1. **Aesthetics.** Does this read as a considered piece of design or as a
   developer's default? Type scale, weight and rhythm; spacing and density;
   the card-on-card nesting; how the plate accent is used and whether it earns
   its place. Tell me what to change, concretely — not "increase contrast" but
   which element, to what.
2. **The workout loop.** `05` and `06` are the screen I stand in front of a
   machine holding. Judge it for: thumb reach on a 6-inch phone, target sizes,
   how few taps a set costs, whether the next action is obvious without
   reading, whether the weight/reps steppers are the right control at all, and
   whether anything on that screen is noise while I am breathing hard. Rest
   timing, set history and the "how did that set feel" prompt are all in play —
   say what should be promoted, demoted or cut.
3. **Onboarding and first run.** `00`–`02`. The first screen asks where I
   train, what equipment exists, how many days, how long, what I enjoy, then
   offers a built-in week, then explains the LLM import, then a safety note.
   That is a lot before anyone has lifted anything. Show me a sequence that
   gets someone to their first logged set faster without losing the answers
   that make a generated plan good — including which questions can wait until
   after the first session, or be inferred.
4. **Information architecture.** Three tabs: Today, History, Plan. The Plan
   tab has become everything that is not a workout or a chart — rotation, gym
   questions, plan library, prompt inputs, import, profile, settings, colour
   key, backup and account. Is three the right number of tabs, and is that the
   right split?
5. **History.** Does it answer "am I getting stronger" at a glance? The trend
   chart is one movement at a time, chosen from a picker.
6. **Light and dark parity.** `12` against `04`. Anything that is weaker in
   one palette than the other, and whether darkening the plate accents for the
   light palette was the right call or a compromise to undo.
7. **Accessibility, honestly.** Contrast in both palettes, tap targets, what
   happens at large text sizes, and whether colour is ever the only carrier of
   meaning — the plate colours are load, which is currently also stated in
   text, but check.

### Constraints — work inside these

- No server-side features in the workout path, and no accounts requirement.
  Offline has to keep working.
- Hand-written HTML, CSS and TypeScript with no UI framework and no component
  library. Design to that: CSS, SVG and a few hundred lines of DOM code are
  cheap; a React-shaped answer is not usable.
- Every colour comes from a design token, so palette-level changes are easy
  and worth proposing.
- Keep the gym-equipment identity unless you can argue properly for replacing
  it. If you would drop the per-day plate accent, say what replaces it.
- It is a personal training log, not a social or coaching product. No feeds,
  streaks-as-guilt, or engagement mechanics.

### What I want back

- **The three worst problems first**, each with the screen it is on, why it
  hurts, and the specific fix.
- **A redesign of the mid-workout screen**, described precisely enough to
  build: layout, hierarchy, what moves, what goes, sizes and spacing where
  they matter.
- **A first-run flow** as a short sequence of screens, with the questions that
  survive and where the rest go.
- **A tightened visual language**: type scale, spacing scale, card treatment,
  accent rules, stated as values I can put into tokens.
- Anything you would cut. This app has more features than screens, and I would
  rather remove than rearrange.

Rough sketches, ASCII layouts or an HTML mockup of any screen are all welcome.
Prioritise depth on the workout loop and the first run over breadth across
every card.
